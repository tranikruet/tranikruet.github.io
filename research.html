Best Track PaperPublished

A Sensor-Based Posture Detection System Towards Ergonomic Health Improvement

Shah Md Tasrifur Rahim, Md. Atiur Rahman, Idrak Al Rakin, Shrabon Mitro, Taufique Ahammed, Rahnuma Islam, Sonia Akhter, Md. Limonur Rahman Lingkon

Proceedings of the 8th IEOM Bangladesh International Conference on Industrial Engineering and Operations Management, Dhaka, December 2025. DOI: 10.46254/BA08.20250059

  1. Research problem

    Long hours on smartphones, tablets, and computers contribute to neck and back pain. Continuous monitoring and timely correction of sitting posture can lower musculoskeletal discomfort.

  2. Methodology

    A waist-worn smart belt carries an MPU-6050 inertial measurement unit (tri-axial accelerometer and gyroscope) connected to an ESP32 microcontroller. A vibration motor gives corrective feedback, and data travels over Wi-Fi to a cloud server and Android and iOS apps.

  3. Computational approach

    IMU data are sampled at 10 Hz and fused with a complementary filter:

    θt = α(θt−1 + ω·Δt) + (1 − α)θacc

    A deviation of more than 30° from a calibrated upright baseline for more than 5 seconds is classified as bad posture.

  4. Results

    Five participants produced about 19,500 samples across structured and natural sitting sessions. Threshold-based classification exceeded 90% accuracy for every posture: upright about 96%, slouched about 94%, right lean about 91%, and left lean about 92%.

  5. Contribution

    A relatively inexpensive, easy-to-use system that combines wearable sensing, real-time haptic feedback, and cloud and mobile analytics to build ergonomic awareness. The paper outlines next steps: adaptive thresholds, signal smoothing, and a second IMU for forward-head posture.

PublishedCo-author

Development of an Innovative Wheelchair-Bed for Enhanced Patient Care in Bangladesh: Integrating Comfort and Accessibility Features

Rupok Islam Avi, R. Rafiul Haque Ayon, Md. Ariful Haque, Shahed Mahmud, Idrak Al Rakin, Shah Md Tasrifur Rahim, Md Fardin Islam

International Conference on Mechanical, Industrial and Materials Engineering 2024 (ICMIME 2024), RUET, Rajshahi, December 2024. Paper ID: ICMIME2024_321

  1. Research problem

    Patients in Bangladesh who are unable to move need assistive devices with comfort and accessibility features that current products lack.

  2. Methodology

    A customer survey of 105 participants, Kano model analysis, a function structure diagram, Quality Function Deployment (QFD), and 3D modeling with stress analysis.

  3. Analytical approach

    Continuous and discrete Kano analyses classified candidate features, and the QFD House of Quality translated customer requirements into technical specifications.

  4. Findings

    The 180-degree recline, defecation system, leg, arm, and headrest, and cleaning system were classified as must-be features, and the cup holder as attractive. Under an 800 N load, the highest frame stress (1.125 × 108 N/m²) stayed below the material's yield strength (3.516 × 108 N/m²).

  5. Contribution

    A wheelchair-bed design aligned with customer requirements and competitive analysis, integrating a 180-degree reclining feature and a commode facility.

Manuscript under final revision

Decision Support System for Supplier Selection: A Comprehensive Approach

Shah Md Tasrifur Rahim et al.

Abstract

Sustainable supplier selection increasingly requires balancing conventional operational performance against environmental responsibility, circular economy practices, and supply chain resilience, yet most multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) methods evaluate criteria as independent and offer limited computational infrastructure for repeatable, practitioner-facing use. This study addresses both gaps by developing a Graph Theory and Matrix Approach (GTMA) based Decision Support System (DSS) that explicitly models directional interdependencies among evaluation criteria while structuring supplier assessment into four criteria groups: conventional, environmental, circular economy, and resilience. The framework assigns supplier-specific performance scores to the diagonal elements of category-level structural matrices and directional inter-criteria influence to the off-diagonal elements, governed by a constant-sum reciprocity rule. A hierarchical two-stage permanent computation using Ryser’s formula with Gray-code-ordered evaluation for computational efficiency first derives category-level GTMA indices and then aggregates them into a final Supplier Performance Index (SPI), yielding a transparent and fully traceable ranking mechanism. The framework is validated through a real manufacturing case study (five supplier alternatives, five-expert panel) using a representative 20-criteria subset, and further tested at full scale using the complete 43-criterion hierarchy to assess computational and structural scalability. Across both evaluations, Supplier B is consistently ranked highest, driven by superior environmental and circular economy performance, while Supplier C leads in conventional and resilience dimensions but trails overall, which demonstrates the framework’s capacity to surface category-level trade-offs that aggregate rankings alone would obscure. Robustness is confirmed through 26 perturbation scenarios spanning diagonal, off-diagonal, and simultaneous adjustments of ±10% and ±20%, with no rank reversals occurring and Spearman rank correlation with the base case remaining 1.000 in every scenario. The proposed DSS thus offers procurement decision makers a scalable, computationally transparent, and empirically robust tool for sustainability oriented supplier evaluation, with category-level diagnostics that support not only final selection but also targeted supplier development.

A Port-Inventory Routing Problem with Route Preprocessing: Application to LPG Distribution in Bangladesh

Undergraduate thesis by Shah Md Tasrifur Rahim, supervised by Sonia Akhter, Associate Professor, Department of Industrial & Production Engineering, RUET.

Abstract

Bangladesh’s autogas sector has expanded rapidly in recent years, placing growing pressure on the inland logistics network that moves liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) from import terminals to end customers. Existing inventory routing studies typically treat port unloading, inventory replenishment, and vehicle routing as separate decisions, an approach that leaves substantial cost and coordination inefficiencies unaddressed in practice. This study formulates a Port-Inventory Routing Problem (P-IRP) that embeds Vendor-Managed Inventory (VMI) within a single multi-period optimization model, jointly determining port unloading schedules, customer inventory levels, route selection, heterogeneous truck assignment, and delivery timing. To keep the resulting Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) model tractable, a route preprocessing procedure generates a restricted but operationally relevant set of candidate delivery routes prior to optimization. The model is applied to a 28-day case instance drawn from a Bangladeshi LPG distributor, spanning two import ports, twenty customers, and two truck types, and solved to near-optimality (MIP gaps of 2.9–6.6%) in Gurobi. Relative to a direct-delivery baseline, the proposed route library reduces truck dispatches by 11.7% and raises capacity-weighted truck utilization from 93.5% to 97.4%, while lowering total logistics cost by 1.2%. Sensitivity analysis further shows that a 10% increase in truck capacity yields a 7.3% cost reduction and an 11.8% drop in dispatches without route-library expansion, underscoring fleet configuration as a high-leverage lever. These findings offer a practical decision-support framework for LPG distributors operating under port-inventory-routing constraints, with implications for logistics planning in comparable energy-distribution contexts.
